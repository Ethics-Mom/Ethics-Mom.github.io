# Psychological Drives: A basis for psychic motion

## Preface

A hobby of mine is making TTRPG rules, weather it be homebrew mechanics for D&D or GURPS, or even from scratch rules (usually inspired by D&D or GURPS). 
After a recent playing of Disco Elysium (Genuinely incredible game, pirate it immediately (in short: corporate drama)) I felt inspired to create a TTRPG rule set that has a great focus on the mental side.
I really liked how GURPS has many out of combat skills, and I really like the challenge of making an interesting campaign that has no combat.
So before laying down any mechanics I really needed to figure out how I'm going to handle the aspects of the mind.

## Categorization

As I go forward with this project I want to lay out some categories.
The mind and body are connected on a spectrum, so I will have to draw some lines.  

Category 1: Psychic Drives  
Category 2: Psychophysical Capabilities  
Category 3: Point Bars  
Category 4: Skills  

Psychic drives are the base driving forces of the mind, if you ask "why did I think/do this?", if you take a mental phenomenon and ask "what's behind this" you should eventually reach a drive.  
The capabilities are similar to the skills in Disco Elysium. They're the tools the mind and body use to act and think.  
Point bars are pretty clear, stuff like Health Points and Fatigue Points.  
And skill, which is technical, specific skills, similar to the D&D and GURPS skill list.

## Inspiration

Of course there's really only one real point to start with in terms of Psychological Drives, and that's Freud!
His two main drives are Thanatos (the death drive) and the Eros (the life drive). 
While serving as a solid and inspirational basis (ignoring the Oedipean parts of his work) it does seem a bit reductive.
So I went and tried to make my own system through iteration, introspection, and discussing it with others to see what their experience is like.

## My Psychological Drives

The Drives form the central force that drives decisions and emotions, While Phobos may represent fear per-se, it is not fear, It is the internal anima of the lizard brain, that part that expects and peers into the unknown.
This applies to the other drives.
The following Drives are ordered from lowest to highest in terms of body to mind.

### Eros (The Bodily Drive)

Eros is the bodily desires, hunger, sexual feelings, and entertainment. It is also the desire to do nothing and to stay asleep.  
At the core of Eros is the body and it's demands.

### Mania (The Hubris Drive)

Mania is the aggressive drive, the desire for power, rage, power-tripping / mania, and power fantasy. It is also the driver of tribalist feelings of in-group and out-group.
Additionally Mania can drive defensiveness in conversation, as well as principals "I am too good for this".  
The core of Mania is the Ego, its defense, and its exertion.

### Phobos (The Fear Drive)

Phobos is the fight or flight response, anxiety, social anxiety, stress, As well as anticipation and excitement.
It also drives the call of the void.  
At the core of Phobos is prediction of what is to come, and of possible futures, as well as the weight of the future.

### Techne (The Diligence Drive)

Techne is the fulfillment drive, the drive to work, to solve problems, to order, to know and learn. It's the part satisfied by craftsmanship and skill, as well as the antsy side.  
At the core of Techne is the nervous energy to do things as human survival instinct to keep moving.

### Psyche (The Enlightenment Drive)

Psyche is on one hand the introspective side that drives feelings around Identity and actualization, and on another hand the social part that empathizes, finds kinship, and romance.  
At the core of Psyche is the innermost mind, the point of view, and the sense of self.

## Interesting Other Points

### Overdetermination

Overdetermination in Freudian theory is when a state of mind is driven by multiple parts of the mind (i.e. a complex being rooted in the id and ego).
In this system Overdetermination occurs when multiple Psychological Drives guide a mental state or action.
For example, a freedom fighter's determination guided by Phobos in fear of the current state of society, Mania in their violence and devotion to an in-group, 
and Techne in taking action that's guided by some political or religious theory.
When overdetermination takes place the resulting mental state can be extremely entrenched and firm, reenforced on multiple axes.

### Possession

Possession in Jungian theory (shout-out Jung) is when the mind is dominated by a single force.
When applied to my system, possession occurs when the minds is completely dominated by a single Psychological Drive.
Phobos possession occurs when the body is entirely controlled by fear, weather that be a panic attack or blindly fleeing or attacking.
Eros possession manifests as wanton indulgence, Mania possession manifests as blinding rage or mania, Techne possession manifests as the flow state, and Psyche possession manifests as enlightenment or bodhi.

### Long-Term Biases

Typically drives switch dominance on a short-term basis, though most people tend to harbor certain drives more often or with greater intensity.
For example, someone with a Mania bias would likely have anger issues.

### Emotional Reactions

When a drive is interacted with in a negative or positive way it tends to illicit an emotional response.
The positive and negative reactions can be seen as the following:  
Phobos forming Excitement and Fear,  
Eros forming Ecstasy and Sadness,  
Mania forming Mania and Rage,  
Techne forming Fulfillment and Antsyness,  
and Psyche forming Actualization and Melancholy.

### Media Analysis

I've found it to be easy to assign these drives to different works of music, image, or movie.
Music in particular really fits well, with most pop songs feeding Eros and more rarely Mania, ambient commonly feeding Psyche, and so on and so forth).
While songs that only exhibit Eros can seem shallow "I love having so much sex and having so much money" songs that only contain Psyche can be abrasive but deep.
More technical and impressive songs feed into Techne, feeding the satisfaction of seeing such skill.

## Silly Stuff

### Application to Wuxing

The Wuxing in Chinese alchemy is the five alchemical elements, which interact in constructive and destructive ways.
I've found that the drives can act constructively or destructively and so I attempted to make a Psychological-Wuxing thing.

![the Wuxing pentagon](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Wuxing_diagram.svg/1920px-Wuxing_diagram.svg.png)

Starting with the solidly known interactions one can quickly assign an order based on the constructive and destructive cycle.
Then you need to align the two, so i chose Phobos-Water as water represents death and it connected well to fear.
This produced the following assignment:  

Psyche: Fire (The spark of enlightenment, the fire of being)  
Eros: Earth (earth as corpus; the body from which desire arises)  
Mania: Metal (Alien, specular, dethatched, powerful, like an iron dagger)  
Water: Phobos (Uncontained, Death, Fate, Future)  
Wood: Techne (Progressive, Overgrown, Pliable, Workable)

The constructive and destructive cycles also fit well.

### As Fictional Godhead

The psychological drives serve well to design deities as a manifestation of the collective or even world's drive, [anima mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anima_mundi).
Many of the geatest deity-like forces in fiction are analyzed by one or multiple psychological drives.  
Two notable examples of Eros-Mania Forces are the Parade from Paprika and the Flame of Frenzy from Elden Ring.  
For the Parade, it is mainly eros through the ecstasy found in the loss of self but has minor elemends of Mania in the way it captivates people into putting their hope in it, joining it as a force, and joining with it to have a share in it's power and splendor.  
The Flame of Frenzy from Elden Ring is primarily manifestation of Mania (taking the decision to end it all, even for others as an act of ultimate hubris), however the tiredness of the Eros, begging the mind and body to just give up is also a key point.
and The Holes from The Enigma of Amigara Fault work well as a manifestation of Phobos, as the unknown it exhibits the call of the void and the intrusive desire to put oneself in danger, Though it also compels like the itch of Techne.  
Granted all of these examples strongly compel as to make their place in their stories important.
